01 · THE NUMBERS
What you should know cold

These are the metrics that separate operators who understand their business from operators who are surprised by it.

Food cost % = Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Food Revenue × 100

Benchmark: Casual dining 28–32% · Full service 30–35% · Fast casual 25–30%. If you're outside your range and don't know why, that's the issue.

Labor cost % = Total labor (wages + taxes + benefits) ÷ Total Revenue × 100

Benchmark: 25–35% depending on concept and service model. The number alone isn't the issue — not knowing it is.

Prime cost = Food & beverage cost + Total labor. The single most important number in your P&L.

Benchmark: Under 60% is healthy. 60–65% is workable with tight control. Over 65% and you are fighting math every single week.

Your break-even isn't just covering food and labor — it includes rent, utilities, insurance, loan payments, and every other fixed cost. If the number in your head doesn't include debt service, it's wrong.

02 · CASH & LEVERAGE
Operating cash flow vs. free cash flow

The most dangerous mistake in restaurant finance: your P&L shows profit but your bank account is shrinking. Knowing why is the difference between managing a business and being managed by it.

Operating Cash Flow is what the restaurant generates from operations — revenue minus the day-to-day cost of running it. Free Cash Flow is what's left after debt service: SBA loans, equipment financing, investor returns. A restaurant can show positive operating cash flow and negative free cash flow. That gap is where operators get into trouble.

Operating expenses = everything it costs to keep the doors open for one week, not including debt payments
Debt payments = SBA loan, equipment financing, investor distributions, any fixed debt obligations
A debt schedule lists every loan and financing obligation: lender, balance, interest rate, monthly payment, and maturity date.

01 · FINANCIAL PLANNING & PROJECTIONS
Do you know what you're opening into?

The financial model you build before you sign a lease will either protect you or haunt you. Most pre-opening operators have one — but most are missing the numbers that actually matter.

A pro forma projects revenue, cost of goods, labor, and all operating expenses month by month. Not a napkin number — a working model.

Most pre-opening models show a P&L break-even — the revenue needed to cover operating costs. The real break-even is higher, because it also has to cover your loan payments. If your model doesn't include debt service, you're working with the wrong number.

Most new restaurants don't hit their revenue projections in the first 6 months. Running the numbers at 70% of your target tells you how long you can survive a slow ramp — and whether your capital structure can handle it.

Menu costing tells you what it actually costs to produce each dish and whether your pricing supports your target food cost %.
02 · CAPITAL STRUCTURE & WORKING CAPITAL
How you're funding it — and what's left after

Being overleveraged going into an opening is the most common financial mistake in this industry. The build cost is only part of the problem. The gap between opening day and cash-flow-positive is where operators run out of money.

Operating Cash Flow is what the restaurant will generate from running the business. Free Cash Flow is what's left after debt service — loan payments, equipment financing, investor obligations. Model both before you open, not after. The difference between the two is your real margin for error.

This is the cash that covers payroll, food orders, rent, and every other bill from opening day until revenue catches up. Build costs almost always run over. Timelines almost always slip. Plan for both.

Monthly debt service over 10% of projected revenue is a warning sign. Over 15% is a structural problem that makes profitability very difficult regardless of how well the restaurant performs operationally.

A line of credit pre-opening is much easier to get than post-opening. Banks lend on projections before you open; after, they lend on performance — which takes time to establish.

04 · BUILD-OUT FINANCIAL DISCIPLINE
Tracking the money before revenue exists

The period between signing a lease and opening the doors is the highest-risk financial window of the entire business. Every dollar out is a real dollar. Revenue is hypothetical. How you track the build determines how much you have left when you open.

Permits, design, construction, equipment, smallwares, POS, pre-opening labor, marketing, licenses, deposits, working capital — every category, not just a round number.
This maps every dollar going out before a dollar comes in — construction draws, equipment deposits, pre-opening payroll, licensing fees, training costs. It tells you when you'll hit your lowest cash point and whether you have enough to get through it.

Restaurant build-outs routinely run 10–20% over budget. A contingency fund isn't pessimism — it's the single most reliable predictor of whether you'll make it to opening day fully capitalized.
