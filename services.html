01OWNER'S REP & CONSTRUCTION MANAGEMENT

Your build, managed.

The architect, the contractor, and the landlord each have their own interests. An owner's rep has one job: protect yours. REI manages the full arc on your behalf — picking the right site, keeping the design workable under service pressure, running the bids and the build, and getting every license and inspection signed before the doors open.

ON SITE & ON PAPER — FROM PERMIT SET TO INSTALL
FEATURED

Construction & pre-construction management

Permitting, scheduling, site meetings, and the hundred small decisions between demo and drywall. The build gets managed so surprises get caught in a meeting, not on a job site.

Contractor selection, bid leveling & contracts

A clear bid package, bids compared line by line, and contract terms reviewed from the owner's side before anything is signed.

Budget, schedule & change-order control

Budget and schedule tracked against the plan every week. Change orders and pay applications are reviewed before money goes out.

Site selection, feasibility & pro formas

Before a lease gets signed, the numbers need to work. Sites are tested against traffic, competition, parking, and build-out cost, and the pro forma shows whether the concept pencils.

Layout & design coordination

A room that photographs well and a room that survives a Friday rush aren't always the same room. Layouts are pushed toward workflow first — kitchen line, service path, bar program, brewhouse footprint — and coordinated with your architect and engineers.

Equipment selection & install

Specs, vendors, lead times, and install sequencing matched to the menu and the volume the space needs, not a generic equipment list.

Licensing & permitting

Federal brewing permits (TTB), state liquor licenses, zoning approvals, building sign-off. Applications and timelines are tracked so licensing isn't what holds up your open date.

Punch list, inspections & opening

Final inspections, punch list, closeout documents, and a clean handoff to the team that will run the room.

WHAT USUALLY TRIPS UP OPERATORS

First-time operators trip on these, and so do long-standing operators getting into the finer points of a build-out. Each is cheaper to catch early than to fix later.

Parking ratios

Zoning can cap your seat count before a floor plan exists.

TI allowance timing

When landlord money is paid decides how much of the build you float.

Store layout

It has to work on a Friday night, not just in the rendering.

Contractor management

Bids, change orders, and schedules need someone watching them weekly.

Support after the ribbon.

THE ROOM, RUNNING — ELICIT BREWING COMPANY
FEATURED

Kitchen additions for breweries without one

A lot of breweries opened without a kitchen and are leaving revenue on the table. Scoping and managing the addition — permitting, layout, equipment, and menu — run as a construction project, for breweries ready to start feeding people.

SOPs, training & opening playbooks

Documented procedures and training that outlast the opening crew, so consistency doesn't depend on any one person still being on staff.

POS & tech stack

The right POS and integrations — payments, reservations, labor, inventory — set up to talk to each other instead of living as separate systems no one fully trusts.

Costing, P&L, menu & vendor support

Menu pricing tied to actual food cost, a P&L you can read at a glance, and vendor relationships reviewed for better pricing and product.
